# مراجعة باردة — عدسة القارئ العربي (Lens A: Arabic closure)

Independent cold read of the rendered Arabic edition at http://localhost:4173, viewports 390×844 and 1440×900. Findings are recorded as encountered; judged from rendered pixels and DOM only.

## Findings


### الصفحة 1 — /ar/ (الرئيسية)

**1. The Arabic is composed natively, not mirrored — confirmed in the measurements.** Page `/ar/`, both viewports. The Arabic edition carries its own type scale rather than inheriting the English one: body 18px/34.2px (1.90) against English 17px/27.2px (1.60); desktop body 22px/37.4px against English 21px/31.5px; h1 30px (mobile) / 42px (desktop) against English 32px / 46px — i.e. the Arabic display size is deliberately *reduced* relative to English, which is the right instinct for a script with no capitals and a tall baseline-to-ascender band. The English negative tracking (`letter-spacing: -0.384px` on h1) is correctly *not* applied to Arabic (`normal`). The face is IBM Plex Sans Arabic throughout, not a Latin face with fallback. Text is `text-align: start`, ragged-left, with no justification and no kashida stretching. This reads as Arabic that someone actually set. Severity: OBSERVATION (positive).

**2. Heading-to-body size contrast is too weak in Arabic.** `/ar/`, 1440×900: h2 is 24px against a 22px paragraph; at 390×844 h2 is 21px against 18px. Arabic has no capitals, no small caps and no true italic, so size and weight carry the whole hierarchy; a 2–3px step at weight 600 vs 500 is close to invisible. On desktop the section headings are rescued by sitting in their own 220px right rail (H2 `w=220`), so position does the work — but on the phone, where the rail collapses into the flow, "ما الذي لا ينبغي استنتاجه؟" and the paragraph under it are nearly the same optical weight. The small gold eyebrow ("إشارات من الأدلة", "السؤال التحليلي") is doing more hierarchical work than the heading itself. Severity: SHOULD FIX.

**3. Numbers, percentages and Latin names inside Arabic render in correct bidi order.** `/ar/`, 390×844, verified by per-character geometry (Range rects sorted by screen x), not by eye. Digits never reverse: `11.9`, `18.35`, `5.44`, `12.91`, `1,473`, `561`, `2021`, `2022`, `2026` all render in their logical order. `11.9%` renders with the percent sign to the visual left of the digits (`%11.9`), which is the Unicode default and the normal Arabic appearance — checked against a control page and it matches the browser default, so nothing has been forced or broken. Latin runs inside Arabic — `Global Findex`, `Global Findex 2021`, `Findex 2021` — hold together as single LTR islands with no fragmentation. Western digits are used consistently (no mixing with Arabic-Indic), and the thousands comma `1,473` and decimal point are consistent across the page. Severity: OBSERVATION (positive).

**4. The publisher is nameable from the first screen, but only in Latin script.** `/ar/`, 390×844, header. The masthead reads, right to left: logo mark, then "CauseWay" over "أدلة الشمول المالي في اليمن". So yes — I can name CauseWay before scrolling. But the publisher's name never appears in Arabic anywhere in the header; the only Arabic form (كوزواي) is baked inside the 40×40 logo bitmap at roughly 8px, where it is unreadable. The footer line "مورد عام … طوّرته CauseWay وتتولى إدارته" also keeps the name Latin. For an Arabic-first reader the publisher is legible but not *named* in their script. Severity: OBSERVATION.

**5. A `<figure>` promises a visual and delivers only its own alt text.** `/ar/`, 390×844 at y≈5052 (figure height 958px) and 1440×900 at y≈3648. The block is titled "الشمول المالي منظومة مترابطة، لا درجة واحدة", poses an analytical question ("كيف ترتبط مكونات منظومة الشمول المالي في اليمن ببعضها؟"), and then contains a heading that reads **"وصف نصي لهذا العرض"** — "a textual description of this display". There is no display. A DOM sweep of the whole page finds no `<svg>`, `<canvas>` or content `<img>` in either language. As a reader I am told I am being given the accessible fallback for a diagram that was never drawn, which reads as an unfinished page rather than a deliberate prose choice. The same hole exists on `/en/`, so this is a product gap, not an Arabic regression — but in Arabic the phrase "هذا العرض" is more pointedly deictic and the absence is more jarring. Severity: SHOULD FIX.

**6. Arabic register and terminology are consistent and idiomatic on this page.** `/ar/`, both viewports. The prose uses real Arabic connective structure ("وفي المسح نفسه،" / "وبصورة منفصلة،" / "ولم يشمل المسح") rather than the clause-by-clause calque a machine produces. The semantic distinctions the site depends on are carried by distinct, stable Arabic terms: الوصول vs الاستخدام, البنية التحتية vs النتيجة, مرخّصة vs تعمل, الحسابات vs الأشخاص, and "قياس سكاني ممثل" for a population-representative measurement. Diacritics are used sparingly and correctly where ambiguity would otherwise bite (نُفذ، جُمعت، تُحدَّث، المُمكِّنة، سدّها). I found nothing on this page that reads as translated. Severity: OBSERVATION (positive).

### الصفحة 2 — /ar/evidence/CLM-003/ (سجل دليل)

**7. Two percentages in the same comparison sentence are typeset in mirror-image conventions.** `/ar/evidence/CLM-003/`, 390×844, section 01, verified both visually and by per-character geometry. The sentence is: «… نموًا نسبته 8.55%، بينما يعرض رسم المصدر لشهر يناير +11%؛ وتُعرض القيمتان معًا بوصفهما تعارضًا داخل المصدر نفسه.» On screen, `8.55%` renders as **%8.55** — percent sign to the visual *left* of the digits, the Unicode default — while `+11%` renders as **+11%** — percent sign to the visual *right*, because that token has been force-isolated to LTR (the browser default for an un-isolated `+11%` in RTL is `%11+`; I checked against a control page). So the whole point of this record — that two numbers for the same month disagree — is presented with the two numbers set in opposite directions, one with the percent leading and one with it trailing. An Arabic reader comparing them has to re-orient between one token and the next. The LTR isolation of the signed value is itself the right call; the failure is that it was not applied to the unsigned percentage beside it. This recurs wherever a signed and an unsigned percentage sit together. Severity: SHOULD FIX.

**8. Section headings are set smaller than the body text they introduce — an English eyebrow convention that does not survive into Arabic.** `/ar/evidence/CLM-003/`, 390×844. Every numbered section heading (`h2`: «01 ما الذي يثبته هذا الدليل؟», «02 ما الذي يقيسه؟» … «07 المنهج وطريقة التحقق») computes to **14px/19.6px** in gold #7A5A1D, while the paragraph immediately beneath it is **20px/34px**. The heading is 30% smaller than its own body copy. In English this pattern works because such labels are set in uppercase with tracking, which reads as a heading at small size; Arabic has no uppercase, no small caps and no italic, so a 14px gold line simply reads as a caption, a source note or fine print — not as the title of the section. Scrolling the record, I could not tell the section titles from the running annotations. Several `h3` in the trust/footer blocks are also 14px. This is the single clearest place where the Arabic page is following an English typographic convention rather than its own. Severity: BLOCKING for the Arabic edition — the document's structure is not legible as structure.

**9. Bidi is correct throughout the record, including the dense numeric passage.** `/ar/evidence/CLM-003/`, 390×844, per-character geometry. `561`, `1,357`, `1,473`, `8.55`, `2025`, `2026`, `26 سبتمبر 2026` all hold their digit order. The Latin source identifiers `SRC-CBY-POS-2025-03`, `SRC-CBY-POS-2025-12`, `SRC-CBY-POS-2026-01` and the record id `CLM-003` render intact as LTR islands — the trailing `-2025-03` segments do *not* reverse, which is the failure mode I was looking for (a bare `2022-2023` in Arabic text reverses to `2023-2022` under the browser default; I confirmed that on a control page). The breadcrumb «الأدلة / CLM-003» and the period «مارس 2025 – يناير 2026» both read in the correct right-to-left order. Severity: OBSERVATION (positive).

**10. The language switch keeps the page but loses the place.** `/ar/evidence/CLM-003/`, `/ar/payments/`, `/ar/readings/same-year-different-number/`, 390×844. Clicking EN from 1500px down the Arabic page lands on the matching English URL every time — `/en/evidence/CLM-003/`, `/en/payments/`, `/en/readings/same-year-different-number/` — so the mapping is right. But `window.scrollY` is 0 on arrival in all three cases: the reader is returned to the top. On a record this is an annoyance; on the long reading (finding 20) it means a bilingual reader who switches to check a phrase has to find their paragraph again by hand. The control is also a `<button data-lang="en">` with no `href`, so it cannot be opened in a new tab and there is no `<link rel="alternate" hreflang>` equivalent a reader can act on — the two editions cannot be held side by side. Severity: SHOULD FIX.

**11. The record's own vocabulary is precise and consistent with the home page.** `/ar/evidence/CLM-003/`, both viewports. «ضمن نطاق الإبلاغ» for reporting perimeter, «مشاهدة» for observation, «قابلة للمقارنة» for comparable, «المجتمع» for population/universe, «يشتقها هذا المورد» for derived-by-us — all used identically to the home page and all correct. The derivation is spelled out in Arabic that a numerate reader can actually follow («بقسمة إجمالي يناير 2026 (1,473) على إجمالي ديسمبر 2025 (1,357) ثم طرح واحد من الناتج»). The disclaimer «الاستشهاد بالدليل لا يعني تلقائيًا امتلاك حق إعادة نشر ملف المصدر» is idiomatic legal Arabic, not a calque. Nothing here reads machine-translated. Severity: OBSERVATION (positive).

### الصفحة 3 — /ar/payments/ (إجابة مجال بعدة أرقام)

**12. The charts are unmirrored English plots dropped into an Arabic page.** `/ar/payments/`, 390×844 (SVG at y≈10362, y≈12931, y≈15598) and 1440×900. Every `<svg>` carries an explicit `dir="ltr"`. The value axis sits on the **left**, and time runs **left to right** (`2025-03` at the left edge, `2026-01` at the right), inside a column whose every other element — the unit label «العدد», the caption, the legend, the data table — is right-aligned RTL. The frame around the plot is Arabic; the plot itself is English. For an Arabic reader the eye enters the figure from the right and lands on the *end* of the series and on empty margin rather than on the value axis. The three POS charts all behave this way. Severity: SHOULD FIX (the decision to keep chronology LTR is defensible; leaving the value axis on the left in an RTL column is not).

**13. Every date inside the figures and data tables is raw ISO, in a page whose prose writes dates in Arabic.** `/ar/payments/`, 390×844. The x-axis ticks read `2025-03`, `2025-07`, `2026-01`; the legend reads «وارد في سجلات إدارية · 2025-03–2026-01»; the second legend line reads «أرقام المصدر · 2025-06، 2025-12، 2026-01 غير متسقة»; and the data table's row headers are `2025-03` … `2026-01`. Two centimetres above, the same figure's own caption says «مارس 2025 – يناير 2026» and the body text says «من 561 جهازًا في مارس 2025 إلى 1,473 جهازًا في يناير 2026». So the same eleven months are written one way for the reader and another way for the machine, inside one figure. `2025-03` is not a date format any Arabic reader uses; it reads as a record key. The tick labels are also only 10.5px. Severity: SHOULD FIX.

**14. One numeric column mixes decimal points and thousands separators so that two values of very different magnitude look identical.** `/ar/payments/`, data table under «القيمة الاسمية لمعاملات نقاط البيع المبلغ عنها شهريًا», unit «مليون ريال يمني». The column runs: `320` · `317.639` · `386.792` · `535.189` · `490.759` · `580.021` · (سبتمبر: «لا قيمة موثَّقة لهذه الفترة — ليست صفرًا») · `795.006` · `783.583` · `910.688` · `1,262`. `910.688` uses the dot as a **decimal** separator (910.688 million) while `1,262` uses the comma as a **thousands** separator (1,262 million) — the two are the same visual shape and differ by three orders of magnitude, in adjacent rows. Precision is also inconsistent: ten months are given to three decimals, and the first and last months are rounded (`320`, `1,262`) with no note. Arabic readers, like most non-US readers, cannot safely assume `.` means decimal; on a site whose whole premise is that a number must be read with its definition attached, this column is not safely readable. Severity: BLOCKING.

**15. The signed/unsigned percentage inconsistency of finding 7 is systemic, not local.** `/ar/payments/`, 390×844, figure «أجهزة نقاط البيع المبلغ عنها شهريًا». Within one paragraph I read: «… البالغ 8.55% (بقسمة 1,473 على 1,357 ثم طرح واحد)، ويُبقيه إلى جانب نسبة +11% المعروضة في إصدار يناير …» and «… يعطي 7.91%، بينما يعرض إصدار يونيو +7.6%.» On screen: **%8.55** and **+11%**; **%7.91** and **+7.6%**. Each pair is the *same comparison*, and each pair is set with the percent sign on opposite sides. It recurs again in the «ما لا يُستنتج» block below. Severity: SHOULD FIX (same defect as 7; recording here because it proves it is a site-wide rule, not a one-off).

**16. The publisher of the underlying data is named in English in the source line and in Arabic in the prose.** `/ar/payments/`, both viewports. Four of the five figures end with «المصدر: Central Bank of Yemen — Aden», while the body text of those same figures says «البنك المركزي اليمني – عدن». The source attribution — the one line a sceptical reader goes to — is the only part not in Arabic. Either form alone would be fine; having both, unlinked, makes the reader check whether they are the same body. Severity: SHOULD FIX.

**17. Figures expose raw URL paths as reader-facing link text.** `/ar/payments/`, both viewports. All five figures close with «السجل الكامل: /ar/evidence/VIS-PAYMENT-ANATOMY/», «… /ar/evidence/VIS-POS-VALUE/» and so on — the bare path, in Latin, as the visible label. The home page does this correctly in the same slot: «السجل الكامل: افتح سجل الدليل ←». So the pattern exists and was simply not applied here. In Arabic the effect is worse than in English: a long Latin path is an opaque LTR block in the middle of an Arabic line and reads as a debug artifact. Severity: SHOULD FIX.

**18. Chart value labels do not collide at either width.** `/ar/payments/`, 390×844 and 1440×900. On the phone the series is thinned to four labelled points (561, 791, 1,357, 1,473) with circled emphasis markers; on the desktop all eleven are shown and none overlap, including the tight 1,357/1,473 pair at the right end. Axis gridlines are dotted and recessive. The missing September 2025 observation is drawn as a break with an explicit legend entry — «┆ 2025-09 · لا قيمة موثَّقة لهذه الفترة — ليست صفرًا» — and the data table repeats the same sentence in the cell rather than showing 0 or a dash. That is exactly right, and it is the strongest thing on the page. Severity: OBSERVATION (positive).

**19. Two minor Arabic composition nits in the figure furniture.** `/ar/payments/`, 1440×900, section 06. The heading «ماذا يعني نظاما FPS وRTGS — وما الذي لا يثبتانه بعد؟» glues the Arabic conjunction directly to a Latin acronym («وRTGS»), which sets as an unspaced Arabic-to-Latin collision; the bidi order is correct but the join is ugly. And in the legend «أرقام المصدر · 2025-06، 2025-12، 2026-01 غير متسقة — يُعرض الرقمان», the predicate «غير متسقة» is pushed away from its subject by the whole date list, so the line has to be re-read. Severity: OBSERVATION.

### الصفحة 4 — /ar/explore/

**20. Link underlines sit too close to the baseline and collide with Arabic descenders and sub-baseline dots.** `/ar/explore/`, 390×844 and 1440×900. Question links are set at 21px with `text-decoration-thickness: 1px` and `text-underline-offset: 3.36px` — about 0.16em. Arabic descends far further than that: the tails of ي، ج، ح، م، ن، ق and, worse, the sub-baseline dots of ب، ي، ج، ز all live in that band. In «ما الذي يتغير في منظومة الشمول المالي في اليمن، وماذا يعني ذلك للأفراد والمنشآت؟» and «من هم الأقل وصولًا، وأين تظهر الفجوات المقاسة؟» the rule runs straight through the tails; `text-decoration-skip-ink: auto` then chops the rule into fragments around them, so the line reads as damaged rather than as a link. The same offsets are applied at 20px, 18px and 15.5px elsewhere on the site. Arabic typography does not use the underline for emphasis for exactly this reason; if it is kept as the link affordance it needs roughly double the offset. Severity: SHOULD FIX.

**21. The desktop RTL composition is genuinely right-to-left, not a flipped English grid.** `/ar/explore/`, 1440×900, measured. The page's content block ends at x=1340 (a 100px right margin). Section headings and the item numbers occupy a rail from x=1120 to x=1340 — i.e. on the **right**, the reading-start edge — and the running text sits to their left in a 584px measure ending at x=1084. The table of contents is on the far left, the furthest point from the reader's entry. The same geometry holds on `/ar/`, `/ar/payments/` and the reading. Whoever laid this out understood which edge an Arabic reader starts from. Severity: OBSERVATION (positive).

**22. Secondary text at 14px is below the size Arabic needs to stay unambiguous.** `/ar/explore/`, 1440×900. The one-line descriptions under each question («صورة مترابطة للمنظومة توضح اختلاف توقيت القياس بين المصادر…», «فصل البنية والتسجيل والنشاط عن دليل الاستخدام المستمر.») are set at 14px in light grey. At that size in this face the dot clusters that distinguish ب/ت/ث and ج/ح/خ and the two dots of ق/ف start to merge, and the reader is decoding rather than reading. Arabic generally needs 1–2px more than a Latin equivalent, not less; the body text on the same page is 22px, so the step down is severe. The same 14px is used for the section headings on the evidence record (finding 8). Severity: SHOULD FIX.

**23. No horizontal overflow and clean wrapping at phone width.** `/ar/explore/`, 390×844. `document.scrollWidth` equals `clientWidth` (390/390) and no element extends past either edge. The long questions — some over 100 characters — wrap to three or four lines ragged-left with no hyphenation, no justification and no stretched forms. The search field is `dir=rtl` with `text-align: start` and an Arabic placeholder. Severity: OBSERVATION (positive).

**24. Item numbers restart at 01 inside every group.** `/ar/explore/`, both viewports. «افهم الصورة العامة (2)» numbers its items 01–02, then «الناس والاستخدام والتدفقات (4)» starts again at 01, and so on, so four different questions on one page are labelled 01. The group counts «(2)»، «(4)»، «(3)» render correctly with the parentheses in the right order. Minor, but the numbers read as identifiers when they are only positions. Severity: OBSERVATION.

### الصفحة 5 — /ar/readings/same-year-different-number/ (قراءة طويلة)

**25. The two side-by-side index panels display literally identical numbers, and one label drops its decimals.** `/ar/readings/same-year-different-number/`, 390×844, the twin charts under «السنة نفسها وقيمتان منشورتان». Panel one — «التقرير السنوي للبنك المركزي اليمني – عدن 2025»، base «2021 · 2,900.22 مليون دولار أمريكي» — is labelled `105.74`، `111.73`، `118`. Panel two — «تقرير خبراء صندوق النقد الدولي لمشاورات المادة الرابعة 2025»، base «2021 · 1,577.7 مليون دولار أمريكي» — is labelled `105.74`، `111.73`، `118`. Same three strings. The prose says the paths are «شبه متطابقين» (*nearly* identical) and quantifies the difference as «0.0032 نقطة مئوية»; the charts say they are exactly identical, which is a stronger claim than the evidence makes. And within each panel the precision is inconsistent: two labels carry two decimals and the third is a bare integer (`118`), so the last point looks rounder and less measured than the others. **26. The all-Latin source line breaks across lines inside a proper name.** `/ar/readings/same-year-different-number/`, 390×844. The figure's attribution is «المصدر: IMF / Yemeni authorities; Central Bank of Yemen — Aden; International Monetary Fund» — three bodies, all in English, in an article that names each of them in Arabic in its own prose («صندوق النقد الدولي»، «البنك المركزي اليمني – عدن»). On the phone the Latin run is too long for the measure and wraps mid-name: «… Central Bank» / «of Yemen — Aden; International Monetary Fund». A long LTR block wrapping inside an RTL column puts the two halves of one name on lines that read in opposite directions. Severity: OBSERVATION (a consequence of finding 16, recorded because the phone width makes it worse).

**27. The figure's "full record" link points at the page the reader is already on.** `/ar/readings/same-year-different-number/`, 390×844. Under the twin charts: «السجل الكامل: /ar/readings/same-year-different-number/». On `/ar/payments/` the same slot at least points at a distinct `VIS-…` record. Severity: SHOULD FIX.

**28. The two source names that the whole article turns on are set at 14.5px.** `/ar/readings/same-year-different-number/`, 390×844, y≈2492 and y≈2852. «التقرير السنوي للبنك المركزي اليمني – عدن 2025» and «تقرير خبراء صندوق النقد الدولي لمشاورات المادة الرابعة 2025» are `h3` at 14.5px, against 20px body. These two labels are the entire point of the piece — which report said which number — and they are the smallest text in the section. Same defect as findings 8 and 22. Severity: OBSERVATION (reinforcement).

**29. The prose itself is genuinely readable end to end on a phone.** `/ar/readings/same-year-different-number/`, 390×844. Body is 20px/34px (leading 1.70), measure 358px, which works out to roughly 43 characters — about eight to ten Arabic words — per line: a good Arabic measure, neither cramped nor sprawling. Ragged left, no justification, no kashida, no hyphenation. Paragraph spacing is 17px on top of 34px leading, so a paragraph break is about one and a half lines — modest but sufficient given the rag. Of 36 paragraphs only one exceeds 420 characters. The `h2` section titles (21px, bold, dark, with a rule above) are clearly distinguishable from body here, unlike the 14px headings elsewhere. I read the argument from «العام نفسه، رقم مختلف» through «ما الذي قد يغيّر هذه القراءة؟» on the phone without losing the thread. Severity: OBSERVATION (positive).

**30. But the reading is 17.7 phone screens long and roughly a third of it is prose.** `/ar/readings/same-year-different-number/`, 390×844: `scrollHeight` 14,955px, i.e. 17.7 viewports. The 36 paragraphs total 6,876 characters — about 5,400px of text. The remaining ~9,500px is apparatus: one figure, five SVG panels, three data tables, the source list, the trace block and two related-links blocks. The argument is repeatedly interrupted by blocks that are reference material rather than reading. On a desktop the side rails absorb this; on a phone the reader scrolls through several screens of table between one paragraph and the next. Severity: OBSERVATION.

**31. Bidi on this page is clean, including the cases most likely to break.** `/ar/readings/same-year-different-number/`, 390×844, per-character geometry. The year ranges «2021–2024» and «2022–2024» hold as LTR islands and do not reverse. The index base «(2021 = 100)» resolves correctly: the bracket glyphs mirror, the opening bracket sits on the right, and an Arabic reader moving leftward gets 2021, then =, then 100, then the close. The IMF report number «26/80», the figures `6,245`، `3,422.16`، `2,900.22`، `1,577.7`، `1.838`، `0.0032` and the percentage `33%` all render in correct order. This is the page with the densest mixed-direction content on the site and none of it is broken. Severity: OBSERVATION (positive).

## الحكم العام — Overall verdict

This is an Arabic edition that was composed, not translated — and that makes its remaining faults unusually frustrating, because they are all in the layer *below* the prose. The writing is the strongest thing here: idiomatic, consistently terminologised across five pages, holding the semantic firewall (وصول/استخدام، بنية تحتية/نتيجة، مرخّصة/تعمل، حسابات/أشخاص) in stable Arabic terms, with diacritics used where ambiguity would bite and nothing that reads as machine output. The type is its own — a real Arabic scale with its own sizes and a 1.7–1.9 leading, Latin tracking correctly withheld, no justification and no kashida — and the desktop grid is properly right-to-left, with headings and numbering on the reading-start edge. Bidi, which I expected to be the failure, is almost entirely correct: digits, Latin names, identifiers, ranges, brackets and report numbers all hold. What fails is everything that was treated as chrome. Section headings are set *smaller than the body they introduce* (14px against 20px) because an English eyebrow convention was carried over into a script with no uppercase to carry it, so on a phone the document has no visible structure; one data column mixes `910.688` and `1,262` so that a decimal and a thousands separator are indistinguishable; the charts are unmirrored English plots with value axes on the left and ISO `2025-03` tick labels in a page that writes «مارس 2025» everywhere else; percent signs sit on opposite sides of the number within a single comparison; source attributions and "full record" links fall back to English names and raw URL paths; and link underlines are set at an offset that cuts through Arabic descenders. None of these are translation problems and none are hard to fix — they are the places where the Arabic edition was assumed to inherit rather than to be designed. I would not call this edition finished while a reader on a phone cannot tell a section title from a caption, and while one published series can be read off by two orders of magnitude.
