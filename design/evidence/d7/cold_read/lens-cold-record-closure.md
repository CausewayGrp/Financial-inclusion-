# Cold-read review — evidence record closure (Lens B)

Reviewer: independent cold-reader simulation, arriving from a search result with no prior context.
Rendered product only, served at http://localhost:4173. Screenshots in scratchpad `lensB/`.
Pages: `/en/evidence/CLM-039/`, `/en/evidence/CLM-044/`, `/en/readings/same-year-different-number/`, each at 1440×900 and 390×844.

Findings are numbered and written as encountered, verbatim from the rendered pixels.

## CLM-039 — /en/evidence/CLM-039/

### Above the fold, 1440×900

What a cold reader sees without scrolling: header "CauseWay / Yemen Financial Inclusion Evidence" with nav (Explore, Evidence, Evidence Readings, Data & sources; Methodology, Measurement Agenda), breadcrumb "Evidence / CLM-039", the label "EVIDENCE RECORD", a dateline "When was it measured or observed? — World Bank public data interfaces as reviewed on 10 September 2026, and earlier documented access", the title "A public interface is not the same thing as the underlying dataset", and section 01 "What does this evidence establish?" in full, plus the start of 02. A numbered table of contents and a "Return to interpretation" block sit in the right rail.

Cold answers at first glance:
1. What is this? — An "evidence record", clearly labelled, about World Bank public remittance data interfaces for Yemen. The genre (a claim record with numbered fields) is legible immediately.
2. Who publishes it? — "CauseWay / Yemen Financial Inclusion Evidence" (header logo + wordmark). WHO or WHAT CauseWay is (company? NGO? research group? where based?) is not answerable above the fold, and only partially below it.
3. What does the record say? — Section 01 states it plainly: one World Bank interface showed an older span of Yemen remittance years than other interfaces and earlier documented retrievals; this reflects display/traceability differences, not missing underlying data. Fully answerable without scrolling on desktop.

### Above the fold, 390×844

Same header (collapsed to Search / العربية / Menu), breadcrumb, EVIDENCE RECORD label, dateline, title, and all of section 01 are visible, followed by the start of the numbered TOC. Questions 1–3 are answerable on mobile without scrolling too. Good.

1. **[OBSERVATION] CLM-039, both viewports — publisher identity is thin for a cold reader.** The header says "CauseWay / Yemen Financial Inclusion Evidence" and the footer says "Developed and maintained by CauseWay. A public resource linking each claim to its evidence and original source." Nothing rendered on the page says what kind of entity CauseWay is, where it is, or who funds it. An "About" link exists in the footer, so the answer is one click away, but on the page itself the publisher is a name, not an identity. For a records-of-evidence site, that is the single most important trust fact a cold reader wants.

2. **[OBSERVATION] CLM-039, mobile 390×844 — the table of contents renders *between* section 01 and section 02.** On mobile the numbered TOC (01–07) appears after the full text of section 01 and before section 02. A cold reader briefly parses it as a repeated list of headings mid-article rather than navigation. It works, but placement before section 01 (or as a collapsible block) would read less like a rendering accident.

3. **[OBSERVATION] CLM-039, desktop — dateline phrasing "as reviewed on 10 September 2026, and earlier documented access" is precise but syntactically odd at first read.** The answer to "when?" is a compound of a review date plus unspecified "earlier documented access" with no dates given for the earlier access. Section 04 repeats the review date but the earlier retrievals are never dated anywhere visible on the page. A cold reader cannot say *when* the "later observations" were seen.

4. **[SHOULD FIX] CLM-039, both viewports — the record's central comparison has no concrete values anywhere on the page.** The record says one interface "showed an older span of years" while others "contained later observations", but never states which years, which interfaces, or how large the gap is. Even one sentence ("interface X displayed data through 2019; interface Y through 2023") would let a reader grasp and check the claim. As rendered, the claim is entirely qualitative and unverifiable from the page; the reader must open the source record and reconstruct the comparison themselves. (If the omission is deliberate policy, the page does not say so here, unlike CLM-044 which does.)

### After scrolling — limits and next steps (CLM-039)

4 (answered). What does it NOT say? — Explicit and well done: section 05 "What this evidence does not establish" ("does not show that data were deleted or withdrawn, or why any correction was made, and one public interface's observations are not a substitute for another's") plus a "Limits of the measure" sub-block. Section 04 "How current is it?" adds that the interfaces may have changed since review. This is the strongest part of the page: the limits are stated in plain language, visually set off, and a cold reader finds them by scanning the numbered sections.

5 (answered). Where next? — Multiple honest exits: "Open source record", "Open original source ↗" (external), "Copy source reference" under section 06; the right-rail/end "Return to interpretation" block linking to the question page ("What do remittances and other money flows tell us about households' connection to the financial system?") and to a reading ("Preserve the meaning of the evidence before comparing the numbers."); Cite this record / Citation and reuse / Corrections & release history / Report an issue at the foot. A cold reader knows exactly where to go.

5. **[OBSERVATION] CLM-039, desktop — section 07 "Method and verification detail" is a collapsed disclosure ("+ Detail for reproducing or challenging this record without changing what it means.")** whose label reads like meta-language. "Without changing what it means" is defensible but strange to an outsider; a plain "How this record was made and how to challenge it" would land better. (Expansion behaviour tested below.)

## CLM-044 — /en/evidence/CLM-044/ (record that withholds its value)

### Above the fold, 1440×900 and 390×844

Visible without scrolling at both sizes: same header and breadcrumb ("Evidence / CLM-044"), "EVIDENCE RECORD", dateline "When was it measured or observed? — 2024 estimate", title "An alternative residual model is not the canonical remittance baseline", and the whole of section 01, which ends: "The estimate considered here rests on material with no publicly accessible original, so, under this resource's publication rule, neither its value nor its producer is reproduced here."

**Verdict on the central question: the page reads as DELIBERATE, not broken — and it establishes that within the first screen, at both viewports.** The withholding is stated as a rule being applied, in the same paragraph that explains the method, before the reader has any chance to form the impression that something failed to load. There is no empty value slot, no dash, no "—", no skeleton, no error styling anywhere. Section 06 says plainly "No public link to the original document is available for this source", and the expanded section 07 says "The material behind this record has no public link to its original document, so no source is listed or linked here and no figure from that material is shown. Read the record as an explanation of method, not as a verifiable figure." A cold reader finishes the page understanding that the absence is the editorial point.

**Leak check (value withheld anywhere on the page?):** I extracted the full rendered text, every `alt`, `aria-label`, `title` and `data-*` attribute, all `svg text` nodes, all zero-size/hidden/`display:none` leaf nodes containing digits, all `<details>` content force-opened, and the page's JSON-LD. **No numeric estimate and no producer name appears anywhere** — visible, hidden, in metadata, or in the collapsed method block. The only digit-bearing hidden nodes are the breadcrumb JSON-LD and a UI string bundle. There is no chart, no table and no export control on this page, so there is nothing to leak through. This is clean.

6. **[SHOULD FIX] CLM-044, both viewports, section 06 — the boilerplate promises a link that does not exist and then contradicts itself.** Rendered verbatim: "Open the source record here, or go to the original document where a public link exists." immediately followed by "No public link to the original document is available for this source." The word "here" is not a link on this record — nothing in section 06 is clickable. A cold reader tries to click "here", finds dead text, and for a moment the page does look broken. On CLM-039 the equivalent line is followed by real "Open source record / Open original source ↗" controls, so the sentence is clearly a shared template that was not suppressed for the no-source case. Either drop the first sentence when there is no source record, or reword it to "This record has no source record to open."

7. **[SHOULD FIX] CLM-044, both viewports — "under this resource's publication rule" is an unlinked reference to a rule the cold reader cannot read.** The single sentence carrying the whole justification for withholding points at a rule that is named but not cited, not quoted and not linked. Methodology is in the top nav, but nothing tells the reader that the rule lives there or under what heading. This is the one place on the page that requires insider knowledge: a regulator or journalist asking "says who, on what basis?" has no rendered path to the answer. A link to the specific methodology section (or one clause quoted inline) would close it.

8. **[OBSERVATION] CLM-044 — the dateline "2024 estimate" answers "when was it measured or observed?" with something that is explicitly not an observation.** The record itself insists this is a model result, not an observation, and section 04 repeats that. The question label above it still asks when it was "measured or observed". Minor, but the template's question fights the record's content on the most prominent line above the title.

9. **[OBSERVATION] CLM-044 — the producer is withheld as well as the value, and the page does not say why the two are treated alike.** "Neither its value nor its producer is reproduced here" will read to some professionals as protective of an institution rather than as a sourcing rule. One clause of rationale ("because naming the producer without a public locator would attribute a figure no reader can check") would remove the ambiguity.

10. **[OBSERVATION] CLM-044 — a "Compare evidence" control appears on this record but not on CLM-039.** It links to `/en/evidence/compare/?records=CLM-044`. Offering to compare a record whose value is deliberately withheld is at least curious to a cold reader; the button's purpose on a value-less record is not explained on the page.

## Evidence Reading — /en/readings/same-year-different-number/

### Above the fold, 1440×900 and 390×844

Visible without scrolling at both sizes: breadcrumb "Evidence Readings / Same year, different number", the label "EVIDENCE READING", a standfirst question ("When two official reports give different remittance values for the same year, what changed: the economy or the measurement?"), the title "Same year, different number", a three-line summary, an "Evidence period" line (Reference years 2021–2024 · CBY-Aden annual reports 2024 and 2025 · IMF Article IV consultation 2025) and "Last reviewed 26 September 2026", then — before any argument — a rule-separated block headed **WHAT NOT TO CONCLUDE**. On desktop the first substantive paragraph with both numbers (USD 6.245bn vs about USD 3.42bn) is also above the fold; on mobile it is one short scroll down.

Cold answers:
1. What is this? — An "evidence reading": an explanatory piece about a statistical revision. Unambiguous.
2. Who publishes it? — CauseWay, same as the records; same limitation as finding 1.
3. What does it say? — That two successive CBY-Aden annual reports give very different 2024 remittance values (6,245 vs 3,422.16 USD million) and that this is a measurement event, not an economic collapse. Clear from the standfirst and first paragraph.
4. What does it not say? — Answered twice over: the "WHAT NOT TO CONCLUDE" block above the article, a "What not to conclude" caption inside the figure, and a full section 04 "What this evidence does not tell us". Genuinely exemplary.
5. Where next? — "Trace the evidence" lists seven evidence records (CLM-032, 036, 037, 041, 042, 043, 044) each with source records; a "Sources" section lists four named sources with "Open source record / Open original source ↗ / Copy source reference"; plus related readings and a right-rail TOC. Strong.

11. **[OBSERVATION] Reading, both viewports — putting "WHAT NOT TO CONCLUDE" *before* the argument is unusual and, for a cold reader, initially confusing.** It opens with dense caveats ("no single scale factor — such as the roughly constant ratio of 1.838 … or the 33% population share the IMF used for areas administered from Aden — is a universal conversion") naming quantities the reader has not yet met. The intent (inoculation before the number) is admirable; the execution asks the reader to hold three unexplained figures before the first sentence of argument. A shorter lead-in caveat with the detailed version repeated at the end would cost nothing.

12. **[SHOULD FIX] Reading, both viewports — the figure's "Text description of this view" is rendered as fully visible body content, tripling the figure.** Confirmed in the rendered pixels and by computed style (`.alt` is `display:block, opacity:1, visibility:visible`; it is hidden only for `body.export-doc`). After the charts the page repeats the entire finding as a prose paragraph, then repeats it again as three data tables — and each of the three tables is preceded by the same 3-line caption repeated verbatim ("Same year, two published values — Reference year 2024 in two publication vintages; indexed paths 2021–2024 — Remittances as defined by each source: …"). To a cold reader this looks like an accessibility fallback that was never hidden, i.e. unfinished markup. The information is valuable; it should be behind a disclosure ("Show the data behind this figure") or visually de-emphasised, and the caption should not repeat three times. On mobile this adds roughly a full screen and a half of duplicated prose between the chart and the next section.

13. **[OBSERVATION] Reading, desktop figure — mixed precision between the two compared values.** The figure prints "6,245" against "3,422.16". Two decimals on one and none on the other, in the same comparison, reads as inconsistent rounding rather than as fidelity to each source. If it is fidelity to each source, the figure does not say so.

14. **[OBSERVATION] Reading, both viewports — the two indexed charts are drawn at identical scales with identical plotted values (100 / 105.74 / 111.73 / 118) and are separated only by a small vertical rule labelled "Not directly comparable".** The charts are literally identical in shape because the underlying index values are identical to the printed precision. A reader who glances will conclude "two sources agree" — which is exactly what the surrounding text says not to conclude. The text does the work; the graphic works against it. Placing the two paths on one chart would make the identity obvious and the warning legible, or the pair could be annotated in-chart with the 1.838 ratio.

## Professional lens — researcher / regulator / journalist

15. **[OBSERVATION] Trust: high, with one gap.** Every figure I saw is attached to a named source with a reference ID (SRC-CBY-AR2025-BOP, SRC-IMF-YEM-AIV-2025-2026 …), a "Corrections & release history" link scoped to the record, an edition date ("Edition of 26 September 2026"), a per-record "Last reviewed" date, and an explicit republication caveat ("Citing the evidence does not automatically grant permission to republish an original source file, table or chart"). The reading distinguishes "Reported by the source" from "Calculated here from published figures" *in the figure's own subtitles* — that is the single most professional thing on the site. What is missing is the publisher's own standing: nowhere on any of the three pages does a rendered sentence say what CauseWay is, who funds it, or who the analysts are. A regulator assessing reliability, or a journalist deciding whether to name the source, cannot do so from the page.

16. **[SHOULD FIX] Citability: a citation is offered but I could not obtain one.** "Cite this record" and "Cite this page" are `<button>` elements, not links. Clicking "Cite this record" on CLM-039 did not change the URL and I could not confirm a rendered citation panel; the same click on CLM-044 produced no new visible text in the page body. If the panel depends on script that is slow or fails, a cold reader gets a dead button on the one control that matters most for citation. **This needs manual verification in a real browser session before release**; on my run it did not visibly respond. Everything else needed for a citation (publisher, record ID, edition date, last-reviewed date, source reference IDs, stable URL) is present on the page, so a determined citer can construct one by hand.

17. **[SHOULD FIX] Screenshot-crop risk on the reading's lead figure — partly defended, not fully.** I cropped the dot-plot region two ways. The looser crop retains "2024 · USD million · Reported by the source" and the interior caption "Same year, different publication" — a reader of that crop cannot honestly mistake it. The tighter crop, starting below the strapline, still retains "Same year, different publication" (good) **but loses the unit entirely**: it then shows two rows labelled "CBY-Aden Annual Report 2024 → 6,245" and "CBY-Aden Annual Report 2025 → 3,422.16" with no "USD million" anywhere. The residual hazard is that the row labels end in the years 2024 and 2025, so the pair reads at a glance as "2024: 6,245 → 2025: 3,422" — a 45% collapse — which is precisely the misreading the whole page exists to prevent. The defence is one small green caption. Recommended: repeat the unit on the value labels or the axis, and label the rows by vintage first ("Annual Report, 2024 vintage" / "…2025 vintage") so the years cannot be read as reference years.

18. **[OBSERVATION] Insider knowledge required in three places.** (a) "under this resource's publication rule" (CLM-044) — unlinked, unexplained. (b) "Structurally aligned on available fields" / the "Compare evidence" feature — offered without saying what comparison means here. (c) Reference IDs like SRC-WB-WDI-REMIT-YEM-CURRENT and CLM-039 are shown as bare chips with no one-line explanation of what a "reference" is or why a reader should keep it. Each is small; together they signal a system designed by people who already know the system.

19. **[OBSERVATION] Clumsy/unfinished, minor.** (a) CLM-039 section 06 carries a hedge that will worry a careful reader: "Some figures in this record are drawn from the source(s) listed on this page; other inputs have not yet been linked to a specific original source. Check each figure against a listed source before reuse, and treat any figure you cannot match as unverified." This is honest, but on a record whose only content is qualitative it is confusing — *which* figures? There are none on the page. (b) The section-07 summary label ("Detail for reproducing or challenging this record without changing what it means") is meta-language. (c) On mobile, the in-body TOC lands between sections 01 and 02 on the evidence records. (d) The dateline question "When was it measured or observed?" is asked even where the record insists nothing was observed (CLM-044).

20. **[OBSERVATION] No layout defects found.** At 390×844 there is no horizontal page scroll on any of the three pages; the charts reflow, type remains legible, the header collapses cleanly to Search / العربية / Menu, and an Arabic toggle is present on every page. Nothing overflowed, overlapped or clipped in any shot I took.

## Final: the honest cold-reader answers, and the verdict

**(1) What is this?** A single, self-contained evidence record (or, for the third page, an explanatory reading) about how Yemeni remittance figures are measured and published — labelled as such, in a numbered seven-question template, legible within two seconds at both viewports. **(2) Who publishes it?** "CauseWay — Yemen Financial Inclusion Evidence." That is all the page tells me: a name and a one-line footer promise. I do not learn what CauseWay is, where it sits, or who pays for it without leaving the page. **(3) What does the record say?** On CLM-039: that different World Bank public interfaces display different spans of Yemen remittance history, and that this is a display-and-traceability fact, not evidence that data were deleted — though the page never tells me which years or which interfaces, so I must take it on trust. On CLM-044: that a balance-of-payments residual estimate of 2024 remittances exists, that it is not the baseline, and that its value and its producer are deliberately not reproduced because the underlying material has no publicly accessible original. On the reading: that CBY-Aden's 2024 and 2025 annual reports put 2024 remittances at USD 6,245m and USD 3,422.16m respectively, and that this is a restatement, not a collapse. **(4) What does it not say?** Each page tells me this itself, prominently and in plain words — a dedicated "What this evidence does not establish" section on the records, a "WHAT NOT TO CONCLUDE" block before the argument and a repeated caption inside the figure on the reading. This is the site's outstanding quality and I have not seen it done better. **(5) Where next?** The source record, the original document (external, where one exists), the interpretive question page, the related reading, corrections history, citation and reuse terms, or "Report an issue" — all offered at the foot of every page and all unambiguous.

**Verdict.** This reads as a serious, unusually self-disciplined evidence resource, and CLM-044 in particular succeeds at the hard thing: a record that withholds its central value reads as **deliberate, not broken**, at both viewports, above the fold, with no leak of the withheld value anywhere in the rendered text, attributes, hidden nodes, collapsed sections or metadata. Nothing I found is blocking. The four things I would fix before release are: the dead "Open the source record here" sentence on records that have no source record (finding 6); the unlinked "this resource's publication rule" that carries the whole justification for withholding (7); the accessibility text description and its three verbatim-captioned tables rendering as visible body copy, which makes the reading's best figure look unfinished (12); and the crop-and-unit hazard on that figure's two-vintage comparison, whose row labels end in "2024" and "2025" and whose values lose their unit as soon as the strapline is cropped (17). The one item I could not settle from the rendered product is whether "Cite this record" actually produces a citation (16) — on a resource built for citation, that button must be verified working.
