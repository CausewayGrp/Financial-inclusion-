Recorded at D3 (27 September 2026). Reader: a fresh agent with no access to the repository, shown only the rendered Home page (`design/reference/out/`, commit before the D3 corrections) as five PNG captures at this language and width, in order; asked what the site is after 30 s, after 90 s and after 180 s, what confused it, what a screenshot could make someone misread, and whether the page reads as serious, trustworthy and clear (Arabic readers: whether the Arabic reads naturally). Verbatim; nothing edited. Findings are classified and acted on in `design/00_DESIGN_README.md` DL-D3-001 and `design/ESCALATIONS.md`.

Reader: a fresh agent shown only the rendered Home page (English, 390 px), screens read in order. Method: opened the five named PNGs with the image reader and nothing else; paused after screen 1 and again after screen 2 to write impressions before scrolling on; screens 3–4 and the full-page capture read last. Caveat on the full-page capture: it is 8,495 px tall and was rendered to me at roughly 92 px wide, so below screen 4 I could make out section structure and some headings but not reliable body text. Where I quote from that region I flag it and the wording may be inexact.

1. After screen 1 only (about 30 seconds)

What it is: a public evidence site about financial inclusion in Yemen, apparently published by "Causeway" (the tiny logo with Arabic underneath; nothing else on the screen says who they are). Site name: "Yemen Financial Inclusion Evidence". The headline "Financial inclusion in Yemen is not one number." tells me the site is a corrective about measurement rather than a dashboard. The first content is one heavily qualified statistic: "11.9% of adults aged 15 and over in the areas surveyed had an account at a financial institution or with a mobile-money provider. Areas holding about 23% of the population were not surveyed."

Why it feels different: there is no hero image, no chart, no big typographic number. The number is buried mid-sentence and wrapped in caveats (fieldwork window, "data year", coverage gap) before I am allowed to believe it. The stat comes in a card with "When was it measured or observed?" and "Open evidence record", so each figure is treated as a record with provenance rather than a fact. Most country-economy sites do the opposite: number first, caveats never.

Where I would start: honestly, I am not sure. The only affordances on the first screen are "Search", "العربية", "Menu" and, in the card, two underlined links: the card title "Latest representative account-ownership measure available" and "Open evidence record". I would tap "Open evidence record" because it is phrased as an action, but I cannot tell whether the title link goes to the same place. There is no "start here". The section label "EVIDENCE SIGNALS" and the subhead "Three figures, three different things measured" promise three figures but screen 1 shows one, so a 30-second reader leaves with a single number and a puzzle. The kicker "YEMEN FINANCIAL INCLUSION EVIDENCE" repeats the header wordmark directly above it, which wastes the most valuable pixels on the page.

2. After screens 1–2 (about 90 seconds)

Scope and boundaries: yes, and they are the point of the page. Every figure carries a "When was it measured or observed?" line and a population line ("Adults 15+ in survey coverage"). The POS figure carries an explicit limit: "POS terminals within the payment-system reporting scope of the Central Bank of Yemen – Aden (CBY-Aden); how far that scope covers each area of Yemen is not established." The bold closing sentence does the boundary work in one line: "These are different measures: people, a disparity within one survey, and infrastructure. None substitutes for another." That is clear and I believed it.

Can it be explored and verified? I am told it can: "Open evidence record" appears under all three figures, and the next section label reads "EVIDENCE RECORDS BEHIND THESE FIGURES". But on screens 1–2 nothing demonstrates verification: no source locator, no dataset name beyond "Global Findex 2021", no CBY-Aden document or table name, no download, no chart. Verification is promised behind a link, not shown. I still do not know what an "evidence record" is (a page? a PDF? a methodology note?).

Things that undercut the boundaries:
- Precision inconsistency: screen 1 gives "11.9%" (one decimal); screen 2 gives "18.35% of men and 5.44% of women ... a gap of 12.91 percentage points" (two decimals). Hundredths of a percent on a survey that skipped areas holding 23% of the population reads as false precision, on a page whose thesis is that numbers should not be over-read.
- Date formats vary card to card: prose says "November 2022 to January 2023", card says "2022-11-07 to 2023-01-09", the POS card says "Mar-2025–Jan-2026". Three formats in three cards.
- Each card has two links (underlined title and "Open evidence record") with no indication whether they are the same destination.
- "in the areas surveyed" / "in survey coverage": the boundary is stated but not located. Which areas? Not on this page.

3. After everything (about 180 seconds)

Which link: as a verifier, "Open evidence record" under the first figure (the 11.9% account-ownership measure), because it is the most concrete claim and the page later says (in "Challenge the answer", read from the full capture, wording approximate) that a record shows the definition, population coverage, calculation base, period, geography, method, limitations and what would change it. As a decision-maker, I would instead tap question "01 Who is being left behind — and where are the measured gaps?", because its subtitle "Population measures, same-wave disparities and what the evidence cannot explain" tells me exactly what I will get.

What I expect there: for the record, the exact Findex indicator, the fieldwork window, the excluded areas and why, the method, the limits, and a link to the original World Bank dataset. For the question, a short answer, its limits, and links out to the records.

Inside within three minutes? Only by the shortcut. The first "Open evidence record" is one tap from screen 1, so yes if that page is a real record. But the designed path ("Understand → Explore → Verify", the "Start with a question" and "Verify evidence" links, the numbered questions) does not appear until screen 4, roughly four phone screens down, and the home page is about 22 screens long in total. A reader who does what the page invites (read the three figures, the non-inference block, the about paragraph) spends the three minutes on the home page and is not yet inside anything. The primary calls to action are underlined text, not buttons, and are easy to scroll past.

Length and repetition (from the full capture, structure only): after the featured reading and "Cite this page" there appears to be a numbered list of the page's own section titles ("01 Three figures... 08 Featured reading"), which looks like a desktop side-navigation dumped at the bottom of the mobile page, and the four record titles seem to be listed a second time near the footer. On a phone these add scrolling without adding anything.

4. What confused me

- "EVIDENCE SIGNALS": a signal of what? Statistic, indicator, warning? Undefined.
- "World Bank reporting year 2022; Global Findex 2021 study; Yemen fieldwork 2022-11-07 to 2023-01-09": three years for one number. A cold reader assumes a typo. One clause explaining why a "2021" survey has 2022–23 fieldwork would remove that.
- "Adults 15+ in survey coverage": jargon; "coverage" could also be read as insurance coverage.
- "within CBY-Aden's reporting scope": I learn a scope exists and that it is "not established" how far it reaches. Honest, but it leaves me holding nothing.
- The fourth card on screen 3 ("Several periods, varying by domain" / "Financial inclusion is a connected system, not a single score" / "people, firms, providers, rails, flows, rules, enabling constraints") sits under the label "EVIDENCE RECORDS BEHIND THESE FIGURES" but is not behind any of the three figures; it is a framing record. The label misdescribes it. "Rails" and "enabling constraints" are insider terms.
- "UNDERSTAND → EXPLORE → VERIFY" heads an about-us paragraph, not three steps I can take.
- "This resource presents the strongest defensible answer while keeping scope, limits and source visible": a self-assessment I cannot test from the page.
- "Questions to start from" / "Start from the problem you need to resolve" / "Choose the question closest to the decision or uncertainty you need to resolve": the same instruction three times in a row.
- The header wordmark wraps to three lines at 390 px beside a logo whose text ("Causeway" plus Arabic) is too small to read.

What a screenshot of the first screen could make someone misread:
- That 11.9% is the national figure for Yemen; the "23% not surveyed" caveat is the last sentence of the paragraph and the easiest thing to crop.
- That the data is from 2021 (from "Global Findex 2021") when the fieldwork was late 2022 to early 2023.
- That this is a World Bank product; the World Bank is the only institution named legibly and the publisher's logo is unreadable at this size, with no publisher line or date on the first screen.
- That "Latest representative account-ownership measure available" means "the only data that exists" rather than "the latest of a series".
- That "Financial inclusion in Yemen is not one number" next to a single number means the number is disputed, rather than that other measures exist.

5. Serious, trustworthy, clear?

Serious: yes. Restrained typography, no stock imagery, named sources (World Bank Global Findex; Central Bank of Yemen – Aden), exact fieldwork dates, a bilingual toggle, and a dedicated block on what not to infer ("Access does not prove use; use does not prove service quality or impact; and the number of accounts is not the number of people"). The footer, as far as I could read it, has About, Corrections, Rights and reuse, Accessibility, Privacy, Terms, which is the right set.

Trustworthy: mostly, with specific reservations. In its favour: it states what it does not know (the 23% gap; the CBY-Aden scope; a section called, approximately, "The gaps the evidence cannot yet close"), it invites challenge ("Challenge the answer"), and it disclaims itself ("It does not make decisions for users or simulate policy outcomes"). Against it: (a) nothing on the home page lets me verify anything without leaving it; every claim defers to "Open evidence record"; (b) I never learn who Causeway is or why they are competent to do this until a footer line "Developed and maintained by Causeway"; a credibility statement belongs near the top; (c) the 11.9% versus 18.35%/5.44%/12.91 rounding inconsistency looks careless on a page about measurement care; (d) lines like "strongest defensible answer" and "helps decision-making" are marketing sitting inside otherwise dry prose; (e) the page length and the repeated blocks at the bottom read as unfinished on a phone.

Clear: partly. The thesis (three measures, none substitutes for another) is stated once, precisely, and I retained it. But the page is text-only and dense; the key number on screen 1 is not visually emphasised; the section labels are the site's internal vocabulary; the two-links-per-card pattern is ambiguous; and the order is backwards for a cold reader: I read three statistics and a disclaimer before, on screen 3–4, I am told what the product is ("a bilingual public evidence resource that helps users understand, compare and verify evidence on financial inclusion in Yemen"). That sentence, or a one-line version of it, belongs on screen 1 under the headline, with "Start with a question" and "Verify evidence" beside it as visible buttons.
