# R4 closure — Sitemap + end-to-end journeys

## Scope and decision method

R4 tested the actual 141-route bilingual product as an information architecture, not as a route inventory. The decision owner was information architecture, challenged by (1) a journalist/researcher verification lens and (2) a regulator/technical-user lens. The session also ran a bounded current-development and legacy-design challenge because a route can be structurally valid and still fail to answer the question a real user now brings to the product.

The Production Master remained the sole semantic/evidence/source/rights/publication authority. The external research and predecessor material used in R4 were challenge inputs only. Where the challenge exposed a public-copy defect that could be repaired from already-admitted evidence, the Production Master was corrected first and subordinate projections regenerated. No current external fact was promoted merely because it was recent or interesting.

## The central IA decision

The product should not become a donor-project portal, a bank dashboard, a 141-item sitemap or a collection of audience-specific mini-sites. Its durable public logic is:

**UNDERSTAND → EXPLORE → VERIFY**

The six-item global navigation remains:

**Explore · Evidence · Readings · Data · Methodology · About**

Home remains the identity entry. Domain routes remain contextual/question-led. Measurement and trust/reuse routes remain contextual/footer destinations. The 108 Evidence Record routes remain stable addressable verification endpoints, not primary navigation.

The important R4 distinction is therefore:

**KEEPING A ROUTE ≠ PROMOTING A ROUTE.**

R4 preserves route identity where deleting it would break citation, source closure, verification, analytical reconstruction or a distinct public obligation, while reducing navigational prominence where the route is not a first-choice entry.

### Route-depth result

- 1 identity/orientation route.
- 6 global-primary routes.
- 19 contextual routes.
- 7 contextual/footer routes.
- 108 addressable verification routes.
- 13 tested end-to-end user journeys.
- 9 bilingual search smoke tests.

No controlled route was deleted. This is not because route count is intrinsically valuable. The Counterfactual Deletion Test found that the apparent redundancy is functional depth: indexes discover; domain pages answer; Evidence Records preserve stable verification/citation identity; Readings synthesize; Compare tests comparability; Data exposes source/reuse context; and trust pages carry distinct public obligations. Removing any family loses material user or evidence-integrity value. The correct simplification is prominence and wayfinding, not deletion of verification identity.

## Material defect found and corrected Master-first

The user challenge on current payment-system developments exposed a real public-journey weakness: the Master already contained FMIIP, FPS, RTGS, access/use support, the 2026 national QR/e-wallet interconnection decision, unified-network integration and YPCC events, but the public Payments and Reforms narratives did not explain what **FPS** and **RTGS** actually mean or how they differ. Search could surface evidence objects, but a non-specialist could encounter acronyms and institutional milestones without a sufficiently clear explanation.

This was not solved with a design note. The Production Master was corrected first.

### Payments now explains

- FMIIP separates a Fast Payment System (FPS), an RTGS/core-banking component, and a separate access/use component.
- FPS is presented as the project’s fast electronic-payment component.
- RTGS is presented as the CBY-Aden-managed settlement backbone the project links to large-value payments between financial institutions.
- The access/use component is distinct: access-point mapping, FI/PSP capacity, merchant onboarding in underserved areas and outreach.
- 2026 QR/e-wallet interconnection, unified-network integration and YPCC institution-building are shown as separate current institutional/architecture states.
- None is relabelled as full go-live, reliability, transaction coverage, active use or an inclusion outcome.
- The use-gap section now makes the FMIIP results framework intelligible as separate measures: operational state, participants, use cases, merchant acceptance, active accounts, customer transaction costs and electronic-payment beneficiaries. Targets remain targets until observed.

### Reforms now explains

FPS, RTGS, the access/use component, QR/e-wallet interconnection, unified-network integration and YPCC are different parts and states in the reform sequence. The page no longer assumes that a reader knows the infrastructure vocabulary before it can understand the evidence-state boundary.

The underlying `31_REFORMS_REGULATION` rows for FMIIP FPS and RTGS were also tightened from the already-admitted UNDP source so the public explanation has a canonical Master basis.

## Current-development challenge — what was used, what was not promoted

R4 checked current official material because “current relevance” cannot be judged from an old IA alone.

### FMIIP / CBY payment architecture — integrated because it was already controlled

Current UNDP and World Bank project material confirms a four-component FMIIP structure beginning in 2025: FPS; RTGS plus CBY-Aden core banking; access/use support; and project management. UNDP describes RTGS as CBY-Aden-managed foundational payment infrastructure and identifies access-point mapping, capacity building, merchant onboarding in underserved areas and outreach. World Bank material also emphasizes interoperability and digitization of cash transfers, government payments and remittances.

The Master already contained these sources and controlled project/result objects, so R4 improved public explanation and discovery without creating a new evidence canon.

Reference challenge inputs:
- https://www.undp.org/yemen/projects/yemen-financial-market-infrastructure-and-inclusion-project
- https://maps.worldbank.org/projects/wb/pid/P180708?status=active
- https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2025/06/17/world-bank-approves-us-30-million-to-support-financial-inclusion-and-education-in-yemen

### IMF 2026 SMP — no R4 expansion required

The July 2026 staff-level agreement is already represented with the correct conditional state. Current IMF material also makes financial-sector intermediation, banking risk management, deposit-taking oversight, audited financial statements and risk-based AML/CFT supervision part of the proposed reform agenda. R4 did not convert that macro/reform context into a new navigation silo or a scorecard because the existing Reforms route is the correct user surface and the current controlled copy already preserves the staff-level/conditional boundary.

Reference challenge input:
- https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/07/16/pr26249-yemen-imf-reaches-sla-on-new-staff-monitored-program

### World Bank CPF FY26–30 — important context, not a new FI route

The 2026–2030 Country Partnership Framework is highly relevant to the surrounding economy: livelihoods/jobs, private-sector growth, women’s participation, institutional strengthening and a whole-of-Yemen operating context. But the CPF is a broad country strategy, not itself a financial-inclusion measurement object. Adding a “World Bank strategy/projects” route would make the product donor-centric and duplicate Reforms, Firms and system-context functions. R4 therefore does not promote the CPF into the sitemap.

Reference challenge inputs:
- https://www.worldbank.org/ext/en/country/yemen/cpf
- https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2026/06/04/yemen-world-bank-group-launches-new-country-partnership-framework-and-projects-to-support-better-livelihoods-and-more-jo

### Digital identity and the June 2026 Cash for Nutrition and Livelihoods Project — named R5 candidate, not silently promoted

World Bank material states that the new project will support more than 675,000 people in obtaining national identity cards and birth certificates, explicitly describing identity documentation as a barrier to financial services and economic participation. That is potentially important to the product because identity sits upstream of onboarding and financial access.

It is **not** inserted in R4: the source is current and relevant, but it is not yet an admitted Production Master evidence object with Source Reference Closure. R5 should test whether the identity→financial-access proposition is incremental to the current evidence base and, if so, admit it properly rather than burying a new fact in navigation copy.

Reference challenge input:
- https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2026/06/30/improving-human-capital-and-building-livelihoods-for-vulnerable-families-in-yemen

### KfW / cash-for-work / MSME finance — literature and programme challenge, not route proliferation

Current KfW material shows continuing Yemen work through SFD cash-for-work/resilience programming and financial-sector support for MSMEs/MFIs. These are useful challenge lenses for the product’s distinction between programme delivery, income support, MFI sector support and population financial inclusion. R4 does not create a KfW or donor programme route. Existing programme-boundary, microfinance and post-transfer-persistence logic already answers the conceptual user problem. R5 should admit any KfW programme evidence only if an original source adds incremental, decision-useful evidence beyond the existing controlled objects.

## International benchmark challenge — what “good” payment-system explanation should ask

R4 used international material as a **benchmark**, not as Yemen evidence. BIS cross-country work on fast payments emphasizes user focus, infrastructure, rules and governance; it finds adoption associated with factors including public-sector role, non-bank participation and breadth of use cases. BIS also defines RTGS as transaction-by-transaction real-time settlement and distinguishes settlement infrastructure from end-user fast-payment services. World Bank social-protection guidance likewise emphasizes open/interoperable payment systems, digital identification and grievance/redress as parts of digital delivery systems.

This benchmark materially improved the product test: the public site should not stop at “a rail exists.” It should make it possible to ask whether there are live participants, useful use cases, merchant acceptance, user reach, costs, reliability, consumer protection and repeat use. The current FMIIP results framework already contains several of those measurement objects, so R4 exposes their separateness instead of inventing a new benchmark score.

Reference challenge inputs:
- https://www.bis.org/publications/qr-202403/fast-payments-design-and-adoption
- https://www.worldbank.org/en/brief/2025/09/19/digital-delivery-systems

No country is used as a “model Yemen must copy,” and no cross-country ranking is introduced.

## Humanitarian / cash-transfer journey corrected

The earlier humanitarian journey was too close to a remittance/macroeconomic-flow journey. That is not the same user problem as cash-transfer delivery.

R4 now tests the path:

**cash-transfer question → After-transfer persistence Reading → CLM-045 evidence record → Payments system context → Data/source verification**

The user is therefore led to the question that actually matters for financial inclusion: a successful payment or newly opened account is not yet durable use. This makes humanitarian/cash-transfer evidence useful without converting programme delivery into national prevalence.

## Legacy challenge result

The archive was used selectively in accordance with its existing disposition register.

Accepted design lessons:
- start with a practical human question;
- question-first entry and progressive depth;
- explicit backtracking from deep Evidence/Reading pages;
- grouped trust/reuse footer;
- screenshot-safe claim anatomy and Arabic-first composition.

Rejected again:
- citizen-versus-expert fact silos;
- uncontrolled calculators and practical advice;
- unsupported household/prevalence claims;
- synthetic indices/econometric models;
- obsolete 29-route IA;
- universal KPI-card templates;
- predecessor visual identity as authority.

The legacy “citizen journey” therefore contributed a **design principle**, not any of its numbers, advice or calculator assumptions.

The search smoke suite now includes stable IDs, population figures, POS, gender gap, Reading discovery, RTGS, Fast Payment System, FMIIP and cash-transfer persistence in both languages.

## What became more true

- The public product now explains the most important current payment-architecture terms rather than merely storing them.
- Cash-transfer delivery and durable financial use are no longer represented as the same user journey.
- The sitemap distinguishes primary navigation from stable verification identity.
- Current international-development activity is used only when it improves interpretation; the site does not become a catalogue of donors or projects.
- Legacy material contributes interaction lessons without recovering factual authority.

## What became simpler

- Six global navigation choices remain sufficient.
- 108 verification endpoints no longer imply 108 navigation choices.
- Payment architecture is explained in the routes where users already ask about payments and reform; no new “payment modernization” route was needed.
- Broad country strategy, donor portfolios and project lists remain outside the core sitemap unless a specific object changes the financial-inclusion evidence story.

## What became more complex

- The Payments/Reforms pages now carry a clearer multi-layer state distinction: rail/component → architecture/integration → institution → operation → use → outcome.
- Search and handoff acceptance now test current payment-rail and cash-transfer journeys explicitly.

This complexity is justified because collapsing those states would be factually wrong.

## What can now be removed or refused

- Any proposal for a second audience-specific navigation system.
- Any proposal to expose all Evidence Records in the global header or a flat sitemap.
- Any duplicate donor/project portal inside the product.
- Any legacy citizen calculator whose inputs/claims are not controlled.
- Any design that treats FPS, RTGS, YPCC, the unified network or QR-wallet interconnection as interchangeable labels.

## Next bounded session

**R5 — Evidence utilization + synthesis**

R5 should not perform a generic literature sweep. It should test whether the current evidence base is being used intelligently and proportionately across the public product: **source/data → public use → interpretation → what remains unknown**. It should identify material under-use, over-use and misuse; close Source Reference Closure across the full public usage set; and adjudicate only genuinely incremental current candidates.

The first named R5 challenge set should include:
1. FMIIP project/result objects versus their actual public use;
2. the 2026 digital-identity/social-protection development as a possible upstream FI enabler;
3. KfW MSME/MFI and cash-for-work programme evidence for incrementality versus existing programme/microfinance objects;
4. whether current payment-system measurement sufficiently captures participants, use cases, merchants/access points, costs, reliability, grievances and active/repeat use without turning targets into results;
5. whether the 2026 CPF/private-sector/women/jobs context materially changes any public synthesis or is correctly kept as background only.

R4 is closed only at the IA/journey level. It does not declare live-release readiness.
