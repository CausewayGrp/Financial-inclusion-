## 0. Bottom line for CWR-012

1. **Neither study measures how money moves between households in Yemen.** Neither gives a channel (hawala or exchange
   company, bank, mobile money, hand-to-hand), a frequency, or a YER amount for informal or person-to-person transfers.
   ISP counts informal "gifts and loans" only as a share of the cash income of aid recipients. SAM gives no transfer
   channel at all. The only mentions are a limitation, that some respondents did not count "hawala" as income, and an
   aid-modality preference question. Both studies are therefore **silent** on the Reading's hypothesis: they neither
   confirm nor overturn it. Missing is not zero.
2. **Both samples are recipients of multipurpose cash assistance (MPCA)** in a few sub-districts under the
   Internationally Recognized Government (IRG). They are not population surveys, and no value in either study is a
   rate for Yemen, for a governorate or for adults.
3. **The SAM multiplier measures "economic activity", not local income.** That activity includes the initial transfer.
   The value comes from one snapshot model, is first-order only, is estimated rather than observed, and has no
   confidence interval or range. The study says its two values "cannot be directly compared".
4. **Best use in CWR-012:** a bounded sentence in the "Humanitarian cash" alternative (records C and D below) and,
   optionally, in "What 'remittance' means" (the hawala-income limitation, record C). The multiplier (record A) belongs
   on a cash or humanitarian surface rather than in this Reading. If it is used here, it can only be context, because
   it says nothing about how money reaches households.

### 1.2 Method

- **Design:** a household quantitative survey that computes a composite index. "The survey designed to measure ISP in
  Yemen consists of four modules, including one vulnerability module and three modules that will be used for
  descriptive/control purposes. These primary modules included questions on demographics, informal social protection,
  and the Food Consumption Score (FCS)." (p. 5 / PDF p. 6). The report shows no KIIs or qualitative component. It says
  instead that "Additional qualitative research is required" (p. 1 / PDF p. 2).
- **Index:** "ISP Index Scores are calculated by summing HHs’ scores for the six dimensions, such that higher Index
  scores imply stronger ISP networks. Each dimension is equally weighted with a maximum score of one, resulting in a
  maximum possible index score of six." (p. 5 / PDF p. 6). The "weighting" here is the index weighting. **No survey
  weighting is stated.**
- **Universe and sample:** "ISP was measured for a statistically representative sample[fn 9] of HHs at the
  sub-district level that received monthly or bi-monthly MPCA distributions from a CCY partner from December 2023 to
  May 2024." (p. 5 / PDF p. 6). Footnote 9: "A statistically representative sample at a 95% confidence interval and 5%
  margin of error, except the Tuban sub-district sample, which is statistically representative at a 95% confidence
  interval and 6% margin of error." (p. 5 / PDF p. 6).
- **Sample sizes:** the unit is the household, and the sizes are printed only on the Figure 1 map: "Al Mudhaffar (125
  HHs)", "Qa'tabah (89 HHs)", "Tuban (112 HHs)" **[image]** (p. 6 / PDF p. 7). No total is printed.
- **Sampling method:** **not stated.** The report does not say whether selection was random or purposive.
- **Geography:** "Al Mudhaffar (Taiz governorate), Qa'tabah (Al Dhale'e governorate), and Tuban (Lahj governorate) are
  located in IRG-controlled Yemen" (p. 5 / PDF p. 6). The Figure 1 map shades "DfA-controlled territory" **[image]**.
- **Period:** "Data was collected in June 2024" (p. 5 / PDF p. 6). **The recall period for income is not printed.**
- **Representativeness, verbatim:** see the sample bullet. It covers MPCA-beneficiary households at the sub-district
  level only.
- **Stated limitations, verbatim:**
  - "the research identified a positive relationship between ISP scores and Food Consumption Scores, meaning food secure
    HHs are more likely to have stronger informal support networks, though the study was not able to draw a causal
    link." (p. 1 / PDF p. 2)
  - "The directionality of the linkage is not measurable using the current study design; that is, whether ISP increases
    household wellness, household wellness increases ISP, or a combination of both." (fn 10, p. 10 / PDF p. 11)
  - "Though it is not possible to ascertain the directionality of the relationship using this survey data" (p. 11 /
    PDF p. 12)
  - "However, the directionality and specific mechanisms underpinning these relationships requires additional
    investigation." (Key Findings, p. 2 / PDF p. 3; repeated in the Conclusion, p. 13 / PDF p. 14)
  - "Reasons why the distribution in Tuban was noticeably different are unclear and must be qualitatively investigated
    once field data collection can be safely conducted in the area." (p. 10 / PDF p. 11)
  - "Additional qualitative research is required to understand the interactions between MPCA and ISP." (p. 1 /
    PDF p. 2)

### 2.2 Method

- **Design:** a social accounting matrix (SAM) multiplier, built from a single snapshot. It is not CGE and not LEWIE.
  - "For this study, a SAM was created for each sub-district using the expenditure patterns of HHs receiving MPCA in
    late 2023 and businesses typically solicited by MPCA beneficiaries." (p. 2 / PDF p. 3)
  - "Computable general equilibrium models (CGE) and its locally focused specification, local economy-wide impact
    evaluations (LEWIE) are frequently used to calculate the multiplier effect […] For this pilot study, the multiplier
    effect was calculated using a single SAM to minimize the amount of expenditure data required to be collected,
    meaning the local economies were observed at an equilibrium state (snapshot in time) and should be interpreted
    accordingly." (p. 4 / PDF p. 5)
  - Equations: "1. M = (I-A)-1 2. y = Mx 3. Multiplier effect = ∑y/c" (p. 5 / PDF p. 6)
  - "The overall economic impact of the cash injection (including the cash injection) is calculated by summing all
    elements of y, and the multiplier effect is calculated by dividing the total economic impact by the total cash
    injection (c)." (p. 5 / PDF p. 6). **The multiplier therefore includes the initial transfer.**
- **Data sources:**
  - Household survey, unit = household: "a representative sample of beneficiary HHs" (p. 2 / PDF p. 3). "which
    resulted in 291 HH interviews[fn 7] in Ad-Dali and 209 in Ja’ar." (p. 2 / PDF p. 3). Footnote 7: "In Ad Dali, 8
    duplicated surveys had to be deleted; therefore, the original sample of 299 HH was not reached."
  - Business KIIs, unit = business owner: "a purposive sample of key informant interviews with business owners"
    (p. 2 / PDF p. 3), "totaling 25 KIIs in Ad-Dali sub-district and 21 KIIs in Ja’ar sub-district." (p. 2 / PDF p. 3).
    "All key informants are business owners except schoolmasters" (fn 65, p. 23 / PDF p. 24).
- **Sampling:**
  - Localities were selected purposively: "Villages and cities that received the most cash assistance in each
    sub-district in the past six months that were accessible by partners to collect data were selected for the study."
    (p. 7 / PDF p. 8)
  - Households were drawn at random: "HHs were randomly chosen from partner MPCA distribution lists." (p. 2 / PDF p. 3)
  - Eligibility: "Beneficiary HHs in these locations were eligible for the study if they received at least two rounds
    of MPCA between July and December 2023, with the last distribution received in October, November, or December
    2023." (p. 2 / PDF p. 3)
  - Precision: "95% confidence Interval; 5% margin of error." (fn 6, p. 2 / PDF p. 3)
- **Transfer value:** "Each HH received 240,000 Yemeni rials (YER) (USD 155.9[fn 35]) per MPCA distribution"
  (p. 7 / PDF p. 8). The rendered page shows "USD 155.9" with superscript 35; the text layer runs these together as
  "155.935". Footnote 35: "According to the following exchange rate: USD 1 = YER 1,539 (mid-December 2023)".
- **Geography:** "selected communities in Ad-Dali and Ja’ar sub-districts of Ad Dali and Abyan governorates, which are
  both under the control of the Internationally Recognized Government." (p. 1 / PDF p. 2)
  - fn 2: "Ad-Dali district in Ad-Dali governorate."
  - fn 3: "Khanfir district in Abyan governorate." The body says "Khanfar district" (p. 7 / PDF p. 8).
- **Data-collection period, which conflicts within the report:**
  - "In January 2024, the CCY conducted a HH survey" (p. 7 / PDF p. 8).
  - Against that: "In both sub-districts, HH data was collected in mid-January 2023 and KIIs was conducted in
    early-to-mid February 2023." (p. 32 / PDF p. 33)
  - Recall: "regular purchasing behavior 30 days prior to data collection and irregular purchasing behavior six months
    prior to data collection." (p. 7 / PDF p. 8)
  - Cash-injection window: "the total YER value of the latest distribution MPCA provided to the assessed communities
    between October and December 2023." (p. 26 / PDF p. 27)
- **Weighting:** **not stated.** The SAM uses medians: "HH expenditure proportions were calculated by computing the
  median expenditure in each category for the HHs in each sub-district" (p. 11 / PDF p. 12).
- **Representativeness and limitation statements, verbatim:**
  - "Multipliers calculating using a SAM assume that prices remain constant, meaning the results are snapshots of an
    economy, which might not provide a representative understanding of economies with volatile currencies and supply
    chains. The SAM multiplier also assumes that the expenditure allocations do not change over time, which is likely
    unrealistic but a basic assumption of multiplier theory.[fn 29] Therefore, the results of any SAM analysis represent
    a particular time period for a geographic area[fn 30] and should not be generalized further." (p. 5 / PDF p. 6)
  - "Business expenditures on employee wages and the proprietor’s income were recorded but removed from the SAM
    calculation because the model did not assess the personal expenditure patterns of business owners and their
    employees, which limits this SAM analysis to the first-order economic impact of cash assistance.[fn 68]" (p. 23 /
    PDF p. 24)
  - "The external expenditure category is excluded from multiplier calculations because external expenditures have
    escaped the local economy." (fn 73, p. 26 / PDF p. 27)
  - "Ideally, we would have assessed HHs that did not receive humanitarian cash assistance […] however, it was not
    possible for this study due to data collection capacity constraints. Therefore, this study does not provide
    insights into how cash assistance influences HH expenditure patterns, but rather quantifies the additional
    expenditure generated from cash assistance via the existing expenditure patterns of HHs receiving cash assistance."
    (fn 33, p. 7 / PDF p. 8)
  - "the study presupposes that how MPCA is allocated across various types of HH expenditures mirrors the overall
    spending pattern of the HH's income." (p. 7 / PDF p. 8)
  - "Representative sampling based on gender, age, population groups, and HH size was not feasible as part of this
    study." (Appendix, p. 36 / PDF p. 37)
  - "the reported expenditure figures should be considered estimates, as they are not hard numbers. Data should be used
    with caution and interpretation, considering the potential for inaccuracies." (p. 36 / PDF p. 37)
  - "Inability to separate in-kind assistance from cash assistance: The study did not account for the confounding
    factor in kind assistance, which may have impacted the multiplier effect value." (p. 41 / PDF p. 42)
  - "The multiplier effect is very sensitive to the markets defined as local and external. Therefore, the multiplier
    effect will be likely be lower when the definition is more geographically constrained." (fn 93, p. 41 / PDF p. 42)
  - "This pilot study only measured the multiplier effect of two local economies in two different time periods."
    (p. 35 / PDF p. 36)

### 2.3 Headline findings: the local multiplier

**Values:**
- "The estimated multiplier effect for Ad-Dali’s was 2.56, which means that every USD 1 of MPCA is estimated to
  produce USD 2.56 worth of economic activity. The estimated multiplier effect for Ja’ar was 2.29, which means that
  every USD 1 of MPCA is estimated to produce USD 2.29 worth of economic activity.[fn 76]" (p. 26 / PDF p. 27). The
  same values appear on p. 1 / PDF p. 2 and p. 35 / PDF p. 36.
  - Unit: USD of "economic activity" per USD 1 of MPCA. This is total impact including the injection, not additional
    income.
  - Universe: "selected villages" or "selected communities" in each sub-district.
  - Period: cash injection October–December 2023; snapshot.

**How it is estimated:** "Therefore, the multiplier effect for the selected villages in Ad-Dali is calculated as
follows: USD 567,689/USD 222,101 ≅ 2.56." (p. 26 / PDF p. 27)

- Table 9, Ad-Dali: "Total Economic Impact USD 567,689.44" (p. 29 / PDF p. 30).
- **Ja’ar's SAM, multiplier matrix, initial gain and answer matrix are not printed.** The value 2.29 cannot be traced

- Comparators are other studies' values: "Lebanon (2.13) […] rural Zimbabwe (2.59), […] Brasil (1.78), and FAO in

- **Exchange-rate preference driver (partner feedback):** "some HH may prefer in-kind food assistance due to the
  constant change of food prices and the instability of the exchange rate" (fn 61, p. 19 / PDF p. 20)
- **Informal credit, households:** "Specifically, about 47% of HHs that received MPCA reported debt repayment compared
  to approximately 30% of HHs that did not receive MPCA within 30 days of the interview." (p. 14 / PDF p. 15)
  - Table 3, "Debt 40% YER 52,500 28%" (p. 16 / PDF p. 17).
- **Informal credit and "liquidity", businesses (KIIs):** "All business owners reported greater liquidity. None reported
  that they extended new credit lines as a result of other credit being paid off, though beneficiaries reported that
  they retained or extended existing credit lines." (p. 31 / PDF p. 32)
  - "Liquidity" here means the businesses' own cash. It is not bank or sector liquidity.
- **Prices:** "The consensus among business owners in both sub-districts was that suppliers did not price gouge when
  MPCA was distributed. In Ad-Dali, four business owners reported that price changes due to YER depreciation limited
  beneficiaries’ ability to purchase supplies." (p. 31 / PDF p. 32)

### 2.5 What SAM does not establish (own words)

- "This study does not provide evidence that MPCA distributions influence the magnitude of the multiplier effect in
  Ad-Dali and Ja’ar; rather, it measures how a cash injection flows through the local economy to generate economic
  activity." (p. 32 / PDF p. 33)
- "The total economic impact of MPCA should not be conflated with permanent economic growth; rather, it quantifies the
  temporary increase in economic activity generated by the cash injection." (p. 32 / PDF p. 33)
- "this study does not provide insights into how cash assistance influences HH expenditure patterns" (fn 33, p. 7 /
  PDF p. 8)
- "the results of any SAM analysis represent a particular time period for a geographic area[fn 30] and should not be
  generalized further." (p. 5 / PDF p. 6)
- "the study does not consider how in-kind assistance might affect household expenditure patterns" (fn 40, p. 8 /
  PDF p. 9)

## 3. Candidate evidence records

### A. CCY multiplier estimate, selected villages of Ad-Dali and Ja’ar

- **Claim (EN):** "A 2024 Cash Consortium of Yemen pilot study, using a single social accounting matrix built from a
  survey of households receiving multipurpose cash assistance and interviews with local business owners, estimated that
  every USD 1 of multipurpose cash assistance produced USD 2.56 worth of economic activity in selected villages of
  Ad-Dali sub-district and USD 2.29 in selected villages of Ja’ar sub-district, both under the control of the
  Internationally Recognized Government."
- **Values:**
  - 2.56 (Ad-Dali) and 2.29 (Ja’ar).
  - Unit: USD of economic activity per USD 1 of MPCA. This is total impact including the transfer itself, and an
    estimate, not an observation.
  - Universe: selected villages and towns that received the most MPCA and were accessible.
  - Period: the latest MPCA distribution, October–December 2023. The household survey ran in January 2024 per p. 7,
    but the report also says 2023 (see §5).
  - Geography: Ad-Dali sub-district (Ad-Dali governorate); Ja’ar sub-district (Khanfar district, Abyan governorate);
    IRG areas.
- **Page:** p. 26 / PDF p. 27; also p. 1 / PDF p. 2 and p. 35 / PDF p. 36.
- **Method limit that must travel:** one snapshot SAM with constant prices and fixed spending shares. It is
  first-order only: business owners' and employees' spending is excluded. External spending is excluded, and the result
  is "very sensitive" to how "local" markets are defined. There is no comparison with non-recipients, and in-kind aid
  is not separated. No confidence interval or range is published. The report says the two values "cannot be directly
  compared". Ja’ar's matrices are not printed.
- **Does not establish:**
  - a multiplier for Yemen, a governorate or a whole sub-district;
  - an increase in household or local income, or permanent growth ("temporary increase in economic activity");
  - that MPCA changed spending patterns;
  - that cash works better in Ad-Dali than in Ja’ar;
  - anything about how money reaches households.

### B. Recipients' stated preference for physical cash

- **Claim (EN):** "In the same study, asked which forms of aid they would prefer in future, with up to three choices,
  84% of the assessed cash-assistance households in Ad-Dali and Ja’ar sub-districts named physical cash."
  - Built from the verbatim "the majority of HHs preferred physical cash (84%)" and the Figure 11 caption "(HHs could
    choose up to three responses)".
- **Values:**
  - 84%.
  - Unit: percent of assessed households, pooled across the two sub-districts. The base is not printed; the sample
    sizes were 291 and 209 household interviews.
  - Period: household survey, January 2024 (p. 7), with the conflict noted in §5.
  - Geography: the selected localities of both sub-districts.
- **Page:** p. 19 / PDF p. 20 (text); Figure 11 on p. 22 / PDF p. 23.
- **Method limit that must travel:**
  - It is a stated preference among aid recipients, asked by agency enumerators, with multiple answers allowed.
  - "Cash via bank transfer" and "Cash via mobile money" were options, but no values are printed for them.
  - The figure is a pooled value, while Figure 11 shows that the sub-districts differ.
- **Does not establish:**
  - how the cash was delivered, or whether recipients use, can reach or hold bank accounts or mobile wallets;
  - any rate for adults or for the population;
  - that physical cash passes through exchange companies.

### C. Main income sources of cash-assistance households, and the hawala caveat

- **Claim (EN):** "Among the assessed households receiving cash assistance in Ad-Dali and Ja’ar sub-districts, the main
  income sources reported for the 30 days before the interview were humanitarian cash assistance (61%) and casual or
  daily wage labour (55%); the study notes that some respondents did not consider hawala or debt to be income."
- **Values:**
  - 61% and 55%.
  - Unit: percent of assessed households reporting each source "on average". The report does not say whether this is
    a pooled share or an average of the two sub-districts.
  - Period: the 30 days before the interview (survey January 2024 per p. 7).
  - Geography: the selected localities of both sub-districts.
- **Pages:** p. 12 / PDF p. 13 (values); p. 36 / PDF p. 37 (hawala limitation).
- **Method limit that must travel:**
  - The sample is MPCA recipients selected for having received at least two rounds, so the share citing humanitarian
    cash reflects how the sample was chosen.
  - Figure 5 includes "Remittances" and "Loans or support from family, friends, neighbours" categories with no printed
    values.
  - Respondents' definitions of income varied, and the study does not state the direction of any bias.
- **Does not establish:**
  - how common remittance receipt is, through any channel;
  - a share of Yemeni households that rely on aid;
  - that remittances are absent: an unlabelled bar is missing, not zero.

### D. Informal loans and gifts in the cash income of cash-assistance households (ISP)

- **Claim (EN):** "Among households receiving multipurpose cash assistance in three sub-districts under the
  Internationally Recognized Government, surveyed in June 2024, assistance made up the majority of household cash
  income, with Qa’tabah reporting the largest share (68%); loans from informal support networks were the
  second-largest share in Al Mudhaffar (47%) and Qa’tabah (26%), and gifts the smallest share in all three sites."
- **Values:**
  - 68%, 47% and 26% (text, p. 9). The same quantities appear to one decimal in Figure 4 **[image]**: 68.2%, 46.6% and
    26.1%. The steward chooses one form and keeps it in English and Arabic.
  - Unit: average percent contribution to household cash income.
  - Universe: sampled MPCA-beneficiary households: Al Mudhaffar (Taiz), Qa’tabah (Al Dhale'e), Tuban (Lahj).
  - Period: data collected June 2024; the income recall period is not printed.
- **Page:** p. 9 / PDF p. 10. Key Finding 1 is on p. 2 / PDF p. 3.
- **Method limit that must travel:**
  - The sample is MPCA recipients only. It is representative at sub-district level of those recipients (95% CI, 5% MOE;
    Tuban 6%).
  - The sampling method and weighting are not stated.
  - Income was "compared that to the amount of MPCA distributed to a HH (YER 240,000)".
  - The four Figure 4 values for each site add to more than 100% (see §5).
- **Does not establish:**
  - informal transfers among the general population;
  - remittances: gifts and loans here are from local support networks, and no domestic or international split is
    printed;
  - the channel, frequency or YER amount of informal transfers.

### E. Self-reported change in giving and receiving after cash assistance (ISP)

- **Claim (EN):** "In Al Mudhaffar, 26% of surveyed cash-assistance households reported an increased ability to provide
  assistance to their informal networks after receiving cash assistance, against 4% reporting an increased ability to
  receive support; in Qa’tabah about 18% reported that their ability to receive support had decreased; in Tuban no
  significant changes were reported."
- **Values:**
  - 26%, 4% and about 18%. Figure 5 **[image]** shows 26.4%, 4.0% and 18.0%.
  - Unit: percent of sampled households in each site.
  - Period: self-report in June 2024, covering the time "Since receiving [your] first cash transfer" (Figure 5 caption).
  - Geography: Al Mudhaffar (Taiz) and Qa’tabah (Al Dhale'e).
- **Page:** p. 9 / PDF p. 10.
- **Method limit that must travel:** these are perceived changes, reported by recipients to partner enumerators, with
  no comparison group and no baseline.
- **Does not establish:**
  - that cash assistance caused the change (chronology is not causality);
  - how much of a transfer is passed on: the report says this needs further research (p. 13 / PDF p. 14);
  - any change in remittances.

Not proposed:
- The YER totals (≈ YER 331m → 846m; 108m → 248m). They are rounded by the source itself ("approximately", "roughly",
  "about"), and they invite reading the result as income.
- The Ad-Dali sector values in Table 9, because of the fn 74 conflict.
- The KII percentages, because of the fraction errors listed in §5.
- The ISP background statistics, which cite third-party sources.
- SAM fn 36 ("64% of HH reported not having any income"), which comes from a different dataset, the CCY MPCA baseline
  for February 2023 to January 2024, with an unstated base.

## 4. Firewall flags

1. **Multiplier read as a national or causal effect.** Values 2.56 and 2.29 apply to selected villages, are estimated,
   come from one snapshot and are first-order. The report forbids generalising them ("should not be generalized
   further") and conflating them with growth. Never write "cash multiplies by 2.5 in Yemen".
2. **Activity is not income.** The study says "economic activity" or "economic impact", and the figure includes the
   USD 1 transfer itself. "Each USD 1 generates USD 2.56 of local income", or "USD 1.56 extra", would be a new and
   computed claim.
3. **Observed, estimated and projected are different.** The multipliers and YER totals are model estimates. The 84%,
   61%/55% and ISP shares are survey observations of a recipient sample.
4. **A share of a surveyed sample is not a national rate.** All percentages are shares of MPCA-recipient households in
   a few IRG-area localities. They are not shares of households or adults in Yemen. The unit is the household, not the
   adult, so they do not compare with Findex.
5. **People are not accounts, and access is not use.** A preference for physical cash says nothing about account
   ownership or access. The bank-transfer and mobile-money bars carry no printed value, so treat them as missing, not
   zero.
6. **Informal support is not a remittance.** ISP's gifts and loans come from informal support networks. Calling them
   "remittances", or using them as evidence for exchange-company channels, breaks the firewall.
7. **Business liquidity is not banking liquidity.** In SAM, "liquidity" means business owners' cash on hand.
8. **Chronology is not causality.** Examples:
   - demand rising "after MPCA distributions";
   - MPCA recipients being more likely to report debt repayment;
   - ISP "Since receiving [your] first cash transfer" (Figure 5 caption).

   In each case the report records an association or a perception.
9. **No comparison across sub-districts.** The study itself says Ad-Dali and Ja’ar "cannot be directly compared".
10. **Secondary claims are not CCY findings.** Background lines cite other publishers and must not be bound to
    SRC-CCY-*. Examples: "banks faced severe liquidity problems", "YER lost about 34%", remittances as "a key source"
    of foreign exchange.

## 5. Reviewer flags: internal inconsistencies

Do not publish the affected values.

**SAM:**
- **Data-collection year.** The report gives "January 2024" (p. 7 / PDF p. 8) and also "mid-January 2023", with KIIs
  in "early-to-mid February 2023" (p. 32 / PDF p. 33). The eligibility window is July–December 2023. Use January 2024
  only with the conflict recorded, and print no KII date.
- **KII fractions:**
  - The fraction and the percentage do not agree in "16 of 25 (66%)" (p. 31 / PDF p. 32).
  - "4 of 21 (19%)" is given for Ad-Dali, where 25 KIIs were held, with no "don't know" note.
  - The text layer runs fractions into footnote markers: "11 of 21[fn 80] (52%)" and "7 of 15[fn 81] (47%)". The
    footnotes explain those two bases.
- **Footnote 74 against Table 8.** fn 74 gives USD 160,468 of direct expenditure and USD 23,590 of indirect
  expenditure. Neither matches Table 8's Food Trader initial gain (USD 133,550.88) or the Table 9 value (USD
  156,769.13).
- **Exchange rate for the totals.** No USD-to-YER rate is stated linking Table 8's USD 222,101.38 to Figure 12's YER
  330,960,000. The only rate given, fn 35, applies to the per-transfer value. Do not derive one.
- **Table 9 label.** Table 9 is labelled "Answer Matrix (A)", though A denotes the SAM and y the answer matrix.
- **Mean or median.** The business-share method says both "computing the mean expenditure" and "summing all categorical
  median expenditures" (p. 23 / PDF p. 24).
- **Place names.** "Khanfir" (fn 3) against "Khanfar" (p. 7). "Ad’ Dhali" (cover) against "Ad-Dali" or "Ad Dali"

**ISP:**
